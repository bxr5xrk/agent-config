# Growth model

Build from one specific economic actor and job. Distinguish user, buyer,
beneficiary, approver, and channel partner when they differ. An ICP includes the
triggering situation, current workaround, urgency, budget, constraints, and why
the product can reach them—not only industry and company size.

Map the loop:

1. **Acquisition:** a qualified prospect enters through a defined channel.
2. **Activation:** they experience the smallest outcome that predicts value.
3. **Retention:** the problem recurs and the product reliably resolves it.
4. **Revenue:** payment aligns with realized value and delivery cost.
5. **Expansion/referral:** usage creates a defensible reason for more accounts,
   volume, teams, or recommendations.

Find the current constraint with funnel data, interviews, sales evidence, or
operational observation. Do not optimize click-through when setup fails, add
onboarding when the ICP is wrong, or chase retention before users reach the value
event. Define activation as an outcome, not account creation or page view.

For B2B, include sales-cycle ownership, champion-to-buyer handoff, implementation
burden, procurement/security needs, and time to first value. For marketplaces or
platform-dependent products, model both sides and the dependency that supplies
inventory, identity, messaging, or discovery.

Prefer loops with a reinforcing mechanism—data advantage, embedded workflow,
collaboration, distribution, or accumulated trust—over a one-time campaign.
Still distinguish a plausible loop from evidence that it operates.
