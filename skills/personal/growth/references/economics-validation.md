# Economics and validation gates

Use contribution economics, not headline revenue. At minimum identify price,
gross margin, variable provider/infrastructure/support cost, expected retention,
sales effort, channel cost, implementation burden, and cash timing. Label unknown
inputs and use ranges rather than fabricated precision.

For B2B validation, evidence strengthens roughly from problem acknowledgment to
scheduled follow-up, access to real workflow/data, time invested by the buyer,
letter of intent, paid pilot, repeated usage, renewal, and expansion. Define the
next gate before building beyond what the current evidence justifies.

A paid pilot should specify ICP, painful workflow, measurable outcome, duration,
price, responsibilities, data/access needs, failure/exit conditions, and the
conversion decision. Discounting is evidence only when the buyer still makes a
real commitment and the delivery cost is understood.

Compare competitors in the category that solves the same job. Verify time-sensitive
pricing, packaging, target customer, and channel mechanics from current sources.
Separate software, agency/service, data provider, responder, and first-message
automation. A large adjacent market does not validate the product's reachable
segment.

Do not scale acquisition while unit economics depend on hidden founder labor,
fragile account pools, unpriced compliance/support, or unmeasured provider
failures. Prefer one falsifiable commercial gate over a longer feature roadmap.
