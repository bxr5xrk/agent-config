# Evidence and claim control

Use a claim ledger when there are several consequential claims, sources conflict,
or the user will make a product, technical, legal, financial, or purchasing
decision from the answer. It may remain working notes; expose it only when useful.

For each material claim record:

- the exact claim, scoped so it can be true or false;
- evidence type: observed implementation, primary documentation, first-party
  statement, dataset/calculation, independent reporting, or inference;
- source location and publication or observation date;
- applicability: product/version/market/account/environment;
- confidence and the strongest contrary evidence or unresolved gap.

Evidence strength follows the claim. Runtime behavior needs runtime evidence;
repository behavior needs the executing path, not only comments; current pricing
needs a dated first-party page or authenticated product evidence; popularity and
outcomes need more than vendor marketing. Search results are discovery aids, not
final evidence. Prefer exact links and line/file references close to the claim.

Reconcile conflicts rather than averaging them. Check whether sources describe
different plans, regions, dates, configurations, or definitions. If the conflict
cannot be resolved, present both scopes and reduce confidence.

Label inference once, near the conclusion it supports. Do not over-qualify direct
observations. Never quote more than needed; paraphrase accurately and retain the
source's important conditions.

Freshness is part of correctness for pricing, availability, platform policy,
company state, software versions, schedules, and recommendations. Verify them
live when practical. For stable implementation history, the current checked-out
revision can be authoritative; identify the revision or working-tree state when
it matters.
