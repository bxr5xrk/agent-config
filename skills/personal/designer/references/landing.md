# Landing and public marketing

Optimize comprehension, credibility and the next useful action for the actual audience. Identify the visitor's question, the honest product promise, supporting proof and the intended conversion. Do not invent customers, testimonials, metrics or capabilities to fill a layout.

Choose an art direction with a reason: product imagery, editorial typography, diagrams, photography, material or a restrained interaction can each carry identity. Build a hierarchy and visual rhythm around the real story; a template's hero/cards/logos/pricing sequence is not a requirement. Let section structure follow the argument rather than repeating one card grid.

Make the product concrete. Prefer a legible, truthful demonstration over decorative dashboard fragments. Distinguish a conceptual illustration from an actual product screenshot. Use real licensed assets or explicitly marked placeholders; do not turn placeholders into product claims.

Balance expressive moments with reading. Keep important content available without a reveal completing; avoid scroll capture and recurring motion competing with text. A signature animation is optional and must earn its cost. Route meaningful motion through the motion specialist and preserve a useful static/reduced version.

For the user's expressive public launch work, develop a memorable composition and purposeful motion when requested or agreed; carry that direction through implementation and temporal QA. Adapt for mobile and reduced motion. Keep heavy 3D conditional on a concrete visual benefit and measured cost; this preference does not transfer to every private/admin surface.

Judge first-screen comprehension, full-page pacing, typography, image crops, proof and CTA continuity at wide and narrow widths. Check where navigation, legal information, forms and footer actually lead. For implementation, coordinate applicable SEO, asset loading and measurement work with the frontend owner; visual polish does not establish that a conversion works.
